In Focus — The Inaugural Lecture
20 August 2026 · AB-1/101 · 3:30 PM

Know Thyself: Cognitive Agency, Experiential Knowledge and Conception of the Self in a Nonhuman Primate

Prof. Anindya Sinha
Professor of Coexistence Studies, TDU
Professor of Animal Behaviour & Cognition,
National Institute of Advanced Studies, Bangalore
About this Talk

In his upcoming talk, "Know Thyself: Cognitive Agency, Experiential Knowledge and Conception of the Self in a Nonhuman Primate" , Prof. Anindya Sinha will explore how complex social dynamics drove the evolution of advanced cognition in primates. Drawing on over three decades of research with bonnet macaques, he will examine their remarkable ability to evaluate relationships, form mental representations, and develop a rudimentary sense of self. Challenging traditional, language-centric definitions of consciousness, this lecture will delve into the philosophical and empirical questions surrounding primate ethology, and will offer a profound look into the conscious decision-making and unique, lived experiences of more-than-human lifeworlds.

About the Speaker

Anindya Sinha is Professor of Coexistence Studies at TDU and Professor of Animal Behaviour and Cognition at the National Institute of Advanced Studies, Bangalore. His research over more than three decades has focused on the behavioural ecology, cognitive ethology, and evolutionary biology of nonhuman primates, and his current work extends into human–nonhuman relations and more-than-human lifeworlds. He was part of the team that reported a new primate species — the Arunachal macaque — from northeastern India, and his writing has shaped how the field thinks about the structure and evolution of the nonhuman primate mind.

§ 02 — About the Series

A gathering for those who study the most complicated three pounds of matter in the known universe.

The Cortex Lectures bring leading scientists in cognition, neuroscience, vision, multisensory perception, motor learning, language, and the philosophy of mind into conversation with our department. Talks are held on the 3rd Thursday of each month at 3:30 PM, with the occasional exception based on speaker availability.

Speakers are chosen by a rotating committee of graduate students and faculty. We deliberately balance computational and experimental, human and animal, theoretical and applied. The result is a series where you might encounter a single-cell electrophysiologist and a Bayesian modeler at the same coffee table afterward — which is exactly the point.

Talks are held in-person at IIT Gandhinagar and are open to the wider academic community across India in hybrid format. Registration for online attendance is limited to Indian academic and research institutions (.ac.in, .edu.in, .res.in). Recordings are archived, with speaker permission, and made available afterward. We welcome speaker suggestions from anyone in our community.
